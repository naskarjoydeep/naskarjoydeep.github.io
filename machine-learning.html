Neural networks as field theories: when an ensemble of networks realizes conformal symmetry, and what field-theoretic structure can be engineered into an architecture.

The area

An ensemble of randomly initialized neural networks defines a distribution over functions, and that distribution can be read as the partition function of a field theory. At infinite width the central limit theorem makes the ensemble Gaussian — a free field — and finite width, or correlated parameters, turns on interactions. This correspondence is useful in both directions: field theory supplies exact statements about network ensembles, and architectures supply concrete realizations of theories that are otherwise defined only formally.

My interest is in the symmetry side of this dictionary. Conformal symmetry is restrictive enough that asking a network ensemble to realize it is a sharp design constraint, and the resulting correlators can be compared directly against what the conformal bootstrap requires.

Questions I'm interested in

  • When does a network ensemble realize a conformal field theory? Translation and rotation invariance are relatively easy to arrange; scale and special conformal transformations are not.
  • Can operators with spin be constructed this way? Scalar correlators are the natural starting point, but the interesting structure — and the sharper tests — live in spinning correlators.
  • How far does the construction go in higher dimensions? Dimension-dependence of the conformal group makes this a nontrivial extension rather than a routine one.
  • Is the correspondence a computational tool or a structural statement? There's a difference between using networks to sample a known theory and using them to define one, and the boundary between the two is worth being precise about.
  • What CFT data can be specified in advance? If architecture choices map onto operator dimensions and OPE coefficients, the dictionary becomes constructive.
