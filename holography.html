Holography

Entanglement structure in AdS/CFT: which entropy inequalities characterize holographic states, and what the geometry of the bulk remembers about the boundary.

The area

The Ryu–Takayanagi formula and its covariant generalization tie entanglement entropy of a boundary region to the area of a minimal surface in the bulk. That correspondence turns a question about quantum information into a question about geometry, and it constrains things sharply: holographic states satisfy entropy inequalities — monogamy of mutual information among them — that generic quantum states do not. The set of all such constraints defines the holographic entropy cone.

Much of my work concerns how far that cone can be mapped out, and what proof techniques suffice to establish its facets. The contraction map method gives a combinatorial way to certify an inequality; understanding its reach, and where it stops, is a way of asking what makes holographic entanglement special.

Questions I'm interested in

  • Is the contraction map proof method complete? If every valid holographic entropy inequality admits a contraction map proof, the classification problem becomes combinatorial. If not, what does the gap look like?
  • Can the holographic entropy cone be classified at higher party number? The number of candidate inequalities grows quickly; structural results matter more than enumeration.
  • What distinguishes holographic entanglement from other structured states? Topologically ordered states have their own entropy signatures — do they satisfy holographic inequalities, and where do the two families part ways?
  • How do mixed-state correlation measures extend the picture? Entanglement of purification and related quantities probe structure that entropy alone misses.
  • What does subregion duality imply about symmetry? Entanglement wedge reconstruction places real constraints on which bulk symmetries can exist.
